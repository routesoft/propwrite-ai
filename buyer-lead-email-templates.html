The buyer lead email that doesn't sound like a form letter

Why 'Just checking in!' gets ignored, and what to send a new buyer lead in the first hour instead.

August 22, 2026 · 6 min read

A lead fills out a form at 9:14pm asking about a listing. By the time they get a reply, it's a subject line that says "Following Up" and a body that opens with "Hi, just checking in to see if you're still interested." They aren't rude enough to say it, but the honest answer is: less interested than they were nine minutes after they filled out the form.

Speed matters, but so does what actually lands in the inbox. A fast reply that reads like a form letter still reads like a form letter.

Why "just checking in" undersells the moment

The lead already told you what they're interested in — that's what the form was for. A reply that doesn't reference the property, the price range, or the reason they likely reached out wastes the one piece of context you were handed for free. It also signals, subtly, that this is an automated sequence rather than a person who read the inquiry.

The first email a lead gets is the only one guaranteed to be read closely. Every one after it competes with a fuller inbox and a cooler lead.

What the first-hour email needs to do

  • Reference the specific property or search criteria they inquired about — not "a listing," the actual one
  • Answer one likely question before they ask it — availability, whether it's still on the market, or a detail the listing photos didn't show
  • Offer one clear next step, not three. A single call-to-action outperforms a menu of options.
  • Sound like a person, not a brokerage. Short sentences, a real name, no "Dear Valued Client."

A structure that works across most first-touch emails

Open with the property or search, not a greeting. Add one sentence of context that shows you actually looked at what they asked about. Ask a single, easy-to-answer question that moves the conversation forward — a showing time, or whether they've been pre-approved. Close with your direct contact info, not just a signature block.

Short example

"Saw you're interested in the 3-bed on Maple — it's still active, and there's a showing slot open Thursday evening if that works for you. Happy to send over the disclosures ahead of time too. What's the best number to reach you at?"

That's four sentences. It references the property, answers the obvious first question (still available), gives one next step, and ends with something the lead can act on without composing a reply.

The follow-up sequence matters more than any single email

Most leads don't convert on the first message regardless of how well it's written. What separates agents who convert leads over time isn't a better opening line — it's a second and third touch that keeps adding something new (a price change, a similar listing, an answer to a question they raised) instead of repeating "just checking in" on a loop.
